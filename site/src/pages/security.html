Security and custody

Your account approves fleet changes. Each device has its own identity, and private releases are encrypted before upload. Here is what those protections mean and what your team still controls.

You hold the keys.

SuiFleet never holds your account key or any device key, and the console never asks for a recovery phrase. SuiFleet holds the capability to upgrade its own smart-contract packages; upgrades keep existing interfaces compatible and are planned, checked against the chain and reviewed before they are signed.

Who holds which key

Use account permissions to decide who may deploy, and access policies to decide which devices may open private releases. These are different permissions. Review every approval request before confirming it.

Your account key

Held by

You: in your wallet (Slush, an extension wallet) or, with Google sign-in, an ephemeral browser key with a zero-knowledge proof issued through Mysten Labs' Enoki service.

Used for

Registering devices, publishing versions, deploying, funding and changing permissions. Changes require the applicable account approval. Browsing and other read-only views do not require a payment approval.

Device key

Held by

Each device. It is generated on the device at first boot, stored with owner-only permissions (with key isolation, readable only by a separate signer service), and is not uploaded by the SuiFleet client. PKCS#11 tokens are supported as an alternative.

Used for

Signing the device's reports, health and log archives, and decrypting releases addressed to it.

Enrollment claim keys

Held by

You, in the downloaded credentials file, until each device uses its claim once.

Used for

Letting a device prove it is the one you reserved during batch enrollment.

Decryption key shares

Held by

Independent Seal key servers, each holding a share; a threshold of them must agree.

Used for

Releasing a decryption key only to identities the on-chain access policy allows.

What the records prove

Release authority

Only accounts holding the right on-chain capability or delegation can publish or deploy. Delegated decryption rights never grant publishing or deployment rights.

Integrity

Devices verify each recipe and artifact against the digests recorded on chain before running anything, and reject versions they do not support.

Device reports

A result or health report is signed by the device's own key and checked against its current registration. It proves which device identity reported, not that the hardware booted correctly or is uncompromised; software keys are not hardware attestation.

History

Transactions provide tamper-evident public history. Live objects and latest health snapshots can change or be retired; older history may require an archival provider. Do not treat the dashboard as an indefinite archive.

Your responsibilities

  • Your platform updater (SWUpdate, RAUC or your own scripts) owns OS installation, partitions, reboots, boot confirmation and firmware rollback. SuiFleet runs the commands you configure and records what they report.
  • Protect the browser you sign in from: a compromised browser or extension can ask you to approve harmful transactions. Read what you approve, and disconnect on shared machines.
  • Keep enrollment credential files out of source control and delete them once devices have enrolled.
  • Remember that on-chain names, group memberships and targets are public. Use confidential delivery for anything proprietary.

Revocation

You can revoke a device or its capability, remove it from groups, close enrollment policies and revoke unused claims. Revocation stops future authority; it cannot recall a release a device has already decrypted.

Security review and deployment planning

  • Choose the installation, recovery and key-protection strategy that matches your devices and deployment requirements.
  • Hardware-backed identity depends on your device’s signer and provisioning setup. Confirm compatibility and validate recovery on representative devices before a wider rollout.
  • Contract security checks run on every change (Move security gates, CI security scanning). No independent third-party audit has been published yet.

Found a problem?

Send security findings to the maintainers privately, without opening a public issue.
