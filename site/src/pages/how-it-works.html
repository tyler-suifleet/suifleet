How it works

SuiFleet is three parts: a static web console, a client daemon on each device, and smart contracts on the Sui network. Release artifacts live on Walrus decentralized storage. None of them requires a SuiFleet-operated server.

The pieces

A release, end to end

  1. Register. The device generates its own key on first boot and prints its address. You register that address in the console, which creates a Device record and a capability bound to it. For volume, a batch enrollment lets devices claim their own records with a one-time code.
  2. Publish. In the console you upload the artifact and a recipe describing how to apply it. The browser encrypts both, stores them on Walrus and records their digests on chain.
  3. Deploy. A deployment names the version and its targets: single devices or groups. The transaction is signed by your wallet, so the target list and the release authority are on the public record.
  4. Apply. Each targeted device sees the deployment, checks its own membership, downloads and decrypts the artifact, verifies its digest and runs your configured install command.
  5. Report. The device signs and submits its result. The console reads results and health directly from the chain and verifies the signatures in your browser.

What SuiFleet does not do

  • It does not install operating systems, choose partitions or reboot devices. Your platform tools own that, and SuiFleet runs the commands you configure.
  • A signed report proves which device identity sent it, not that the hardware booted correctly or is uncompromised.
  • Live telemetry pipelines are not part of the product. Devices publish bounded signed health on chain and encrypted logs to Walrus; you can keep any existing telemetry pipeline.
